Integrations

Built around the security infrastructure you already have

PARVEK reads standard camera streams, runs alongside your existing recorder, and drives the response hardware on site. Everything below carries its real status — deployed, integrated per site, or scoped per site.

Cameras and video

The integration that matters most, and the one with the most deployment behind it. PARVEK reads standard streams rather than talking to a vendor SDK, so the question is the stream, not the badge on the housing.

RTSP streams Deployed

The primary path. Any camera that serves a usable RTSP stream meeting the baseline below.

ONVIF discovery and streaming Deployed

Supported ONVIF profiles for discovery and stream setup.

Existing recorders and NVRs Deployed

PARVEK reads cameras directly or through the recorder, and the recorder keeps recording exactly as before. It is not replaced.

H.264 and H.265 Deployed

Both decoded. MJPEG works but is inefficient for continuous analytics.

Analog cameras Scoped per site

Need an encoder to produce an IP stream first. That is a hardware addition, not a software setting.

Access control (PACS)

We build the connection to your access-control system as part of onboarding, and you watch it run on your own doors during the 30-day trial — before you commit to anything.

Access event ingestion Integrated per site

Reading door events — system id, event id, badge id, user id, timestamp — from an API, a database view or a scheduled export. Built against your system during onboarding.

Event–video correlation Integrated per site

Pairing each door event with the camera frame from that moment, and matching the badge user against the person recognized.

Door control Scoped per site

Not planned. PARVEK reads events and correlates them; locking and unlocking stays entirely with your access-control system.

Response hardware

Where a detection needs to do something physical. These are wired at commissioning and depend on hardware being present on site.

Speakers and voice announcement Deployed

Local spoken warning on a detection, in the area where it happened.

Sirens and horns Deployed

Driven through relay outputs on the appliance.

Floodlights and area lighting Deployed

Switched on a detection — frequently the most effective response, and the one that also fixes the detection problem.

Relay outputs to third-party devices Scoped per site

Anything a dry contact can trigger. What it is wired to is a site decision.

Notification and workflow

Where events land once they are raised. Everything here is outbound only — no inbound ports are opened on your network.

Mobile push notifications Deployed

Alerts with the frame attached, to the people you nominate.

Web dashboard and event queue Deployed

The operations console on your own network, with role-based access per site.

Email reports Deployed

Scheduled summaries and per-event reporting.

Enterprise APIs and SIEM / VMS forwarding Scoped per site

Pushing events into your own tooling. Possible, but scoped per deployment rather than shipped as a connector.

Camera Baseline

What a camera has to meet

This is a compatibility minimum, not a preference. Meeting it does not guarantee good results — the scene, lens and lighting still decide that — but missing it rules a camera out.

Resolution

2 MP / 1080p or higher.

Frame rate

15 fps or higher for general analytics.

Codec

H.264 or H.265.

Streaming

RTSP, or supported ONVIF streaming.

Network

Stable IP network connectivity to the appliance.

Image quality

Usable day and night image quality for the scene.

Frequently Asked Questions

Why is there no list of supported camera brands with ticks against it?

Because compatibility is decided by model, firmware and stream, not by brand. A vendor can sell both a camera that works and one that does not. Representative examples, not a blanket guarantee. Compatibility is model, firmware and stream dependent — we confirm the specific models on your site rather than the brand.

Which access-control systems are supported?

We integrate with the one you run rather than publishing a logo wall. The correlation needs an event export carrying a system id, event id, badge id, user id and timestamp, which most platforms provide through an API, a database view or a scheduled export. Tell us what you run and we will confirm what it can feed before you commit.

Do you need an SDK or a partnership with our camera vendor?

No. Reading standard RTSP and ONVIF rather than per-vendor SDKs is what lets PARVEK work across a mixed estate — which is what most real sites have after a decade of piecemeal purchasing.

What does "scoped per site" actually mean in cost terms?

That it is quoted as part of the deployment rather than included in the subscription. The assessment establishes the work; you get the number before anything is committed.

Our estate is mixed and partly undocumented. Where do we start?

The camera check and the site assessment, in that order. Both are free, and both are designed to tell you what you have before anyone proposes what to buy. If something cannot work, you hear it there. Ask at contact@parvek.ai.

Tell us what you run

Cameras, recorder, access-control system. We will tell you what connects today, what is in development, and what would be project work — before you spend anything.
