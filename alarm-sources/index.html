Alarm Sources

One visual verification layer across your security events.

An alarm tells you something happened. The camera covering that area is what tells you what happened. These are the event sources PARVEK can put a picture against — and, just as importantly, where video genuinely helps and where it is only supplementary.

Event Sources

What PARVEK can put a camera view against

Which of these apply to your site depends on what you run, where your cameras are pointed and what they can resolve. That is what the site survey is for — it establishes the real answer per camera before anything is proposed or bought.

Access control (PACS)

Denied accessInvalid badgeDoor held openAnti-passbackAfter-hours entry

A door event records the credential. The camera covering that door recorded the person. PARVEK brings the two together so you can see who was actually there, match them against the badge holder and keep the footage with the event. What a given access system can hand over is established during onboarding, against the platform you already run.

Intrusion detection

Zone alarmInterior intrusionMotion in a protected areaOut-of-hours activity

An intrusion event tells you a protected area reported something. PARVEK watches the same areas on camera, raises its own event with the frame that caused it, and can respond in that area while it is happening. Where coverage exists, the camera view is what turns a state change into a decision.

Perimeter

Fence line crossingGate alarmYard and boundary entryVehicle at the perimeter

Boundaries are drawn on the camera view with the hours they are off limits. A person inside one becomes an event with the frame attached, and the area can be lit and challenged over the speaker. What a camera can resolve at the distance involved, especially after dark, is the thing that decides whether this works — which is what the survey measures.

Operational and industrial alerts

Machine faultConveyor stopProcess-area alertRestricted plant area entry

On a plant, the cameras are usually already there and nobody has the capacity to watch them. PARVEK raises its own events from those cameras — a person in a plant room out of hours, movement on a conveyor run, occupancy in a bay. Bringing in alerts from a control system is worked out against the specific system during the survey rather than assumed.

Building, environmental and IoT

Leak or floodingTemperature excursionBuilding faultPlant room alert

Video is supplementary here, and only useful where a camera actually covers the area concerned. Where it does, the picture tells whoever is responding what they are walking into. Where it does not, the survey says so rather than leaving you to discover it later.

Fire and life safety

Fire panel activationSmoke or heat detectorSprinkler flow

Certified life-safety detection stays authoritative, always. PARVEK is not a substitute for a fire panel or a certified detector and must never be deployed as one. What it can add is visual context around an area during an event, and visible flame or smoke detected in a camera view as a supplementary signal alongside — never instead of — certified detection.

Vehicles detected, counted and timestamped at gates, in yards and across parking areas, each event carrying the frame behind it. Useful both as a security event and as an operational record of what moved and when.

Frequently Asked Questions

Do we need all of these to get value?

No. Most sites start with two or three — usually a perimeter or restricted area, an entrance, and the events that are currently being missed overnight. The survey establishes which ones actually matter at your site rather than switching everything on.

What if our existing alarm system is the one raising events?

Then the useful question is which camera covers the area that system is protecting. PARVEK raises its own events from those cameras, so you get a picture of the area regardless. Reading events out of another system is worked out against that specific system during the survey.

Can video prove an alarm was false?

It can show you what the camera saw, which is usually enough to decide what to do. It is not a guarantee: if the footage is inconclusive — the area is dark, the angle is wrong, the subject is out of frame — then it is inconclusive, and we would rather tell you that than claim otherwise.

Is this a replacement for our fire or life-safety system?

No. Certified life-safety detection stays authoritative. PARVEK adds visual context around an area; it is not a certified detector and must not be deployed as a substitute for one.

How do we find out which of these apply to us?

Start with the site survey. It covers your cameras, their placement and lighting, the areas you care about and the events you are actually trying to catch. From there a proof of concept runs it on your own footage. Ask at contact@parvek.ai if you want to talk it through first.

AI surveillance and analytics are technology-assisted and do not guarantee prevention or detection of every security incident or 100% accuracy.
