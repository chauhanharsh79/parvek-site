Access Intelligence

Your access system knows the badge. Your camera saw the person.

PARVEK is building the layer that puts those two together — matching the user id on the credential against the person recognized at the door, on your own appliance, without either system changing.

The Problem

Two systems. Two halves of the same event.

Your access system records the credential

It knows a badge was presented, which door opened and when. It does not know who was holding the badge.

Your cameras record the person

They saw who walked through. They do not know whether that person was the one entitled to.

Your operators join the two by hand

Export from one system, scrub footage in the other, line the clocks up. It is slow enough that most events are never checked at all.

A badge swipe tells you a credential was used. A camera tells you a person went through. Neither tells you whether they were the same person.

How the Correlation Works

Matched on user_id, on your appliance

Deliberately small for a first version. Read the access event, find the camera detections around it, and compare the person to the credential.

The Event Record

What we read from your PACS

Five fields. Nothing about door control, nothing written back, and no change to how your access-control system operates.

system_id
Which access-control system the event came from, so a multi-system estate stays unambiguous.
event_id
The PACS’s own identifier for the event, so every correlation can be traced back to its source record.
badge_id
The credential presented at the reader.
user_id
The person the credential belongs to. This is the field the correlation turns on.
timestamp
When the PACS recorded the event, used to find the camera detections that bracket it.
The Sequence

What happens per event

  1. The door event arrives

    PARVEK reads the access event from your PACS: which system, which event, which badge, which user, and when.

  2. The camera covering that door is checked

    Detections around that timestamp are pulled from the camera watching the reader — who was there, and how many of them.

  3. Identity is compared

    Where face recognition is enrolled, the person seen at the door has a user id of their own. The correlation is simply whether that matches the user id on the badge.

  4. The event is classified

    Matched, mismatched, or unverifiable — and an unverifiable result is reported as unverifiable rather than quietly treated as a pass.

What It Enables

In the first version, and after it

Split honestly. The first group falls out of the user-id match directly; the second needs that working first, and depends on camera placement as much as on models.

First version

Access event verification

Every door event paired with the camera frame from the moment it happened, so a badge swipe stops being a line in a log and becomes something you can look at.

First version

Badge–person mismatch

The badge says one user opened the door; the camera recognized a different enrolled person. That gap is the signal, and it is what the user-id match exists to find.

First version

PACS alarm verification

A door-forced or door-held alarm raised with its video attached, so an operator can tell a real forced door from a delivery propping it open — without opening a second system.

After v1

Tailgating detection

One access grant, more than one person through the door. Needs reliable people-counting at the reader, which is a camera placement problem before it is a model problem.

After v1

Access investigation

Follow a person or a credential across doors and cameras on one timeline, instead of exporting from two systems and lining up clocks by hand.

What It Needs

Where this will and will not work

These decide the outcome more than the model does. If your site cannot meet them, we would rather say so now.

A PACS that will export events

An API, a database view, or a scheduled export — any of them works. What we cannot do is read a system that will not give up its event log.

A camera that actually sees the reader

Correlation is only as good as the view. A camera that catches the back of a head as the door closes cannot confirm who went through it.

Face recognition enrolled, for identity matching

Pairing a door event with video needs no enrolment. Deciding whether the right person used the badge does — without it, the mismatch check cannot run.

A shared identity between the two systems

The PACS user id and the enrolled person have to be the same individual. Mapping those is a commissioning task, and on a large estate it is the bulk of the work.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is this available today?

No. Access Intelligence is being built now and is not yet deployed on customer sites. We are looking for design partners who run a PACS and want to shape it. If you run a PACS and this is a problem you have, that conversation is genuinely useful to us — and we would rather have it now than sell you something that does not exist yet.

Which access-control systems do you support?

None are validated yet, and we are not going to publish a logo wall of systems we have not read an event from. The v1 design needs only an event export carrying a system id, event id, badge id, user id and timestamp — which most platforms can provide through an API, a database view or a scheduled export. Tell us what you run and we will tell you straight whether it can feed that.

Do we have to replace our access-control system?

No, and that is the point. PARVEK reads events from the system you already run, the same way it reads streams from cameras you already own. It does not control doors and it does not sit in the access path.

Can PARVEK unlock or lock doors?

Not in this design. It reads events and correlates them. Door control stays entirely with your access-control system, which keeps PARVEK out of the path of anything safety-critical.

Does any of this need the cloud?

No. The correlation runs on the appliance on your premises, alongside the detection that is already running there. No video and no access events leave the site to make the match.

What does it need from us to work?

A PACS that will export its events, a camera that genuinely sees the reader, and — for identity matching specifically — face recognition enrolled, with the PACS user mapped to the enrolled person. That mapping is the real work on a large estate.

What happens when it cannot tell?

It says so. An event with no clear view of the door, or where nobody enrolled was recognized, is reported as unverifiable rather than quietly passed. A verification system that hides its own blind spots is worse than none.

AI surveillance and analytics are technology-assisted and do not guarantee prevention or detection of every security incident or 100% accuracy.
