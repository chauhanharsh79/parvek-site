Restricted Zones & Access

A camera records the room. A zone watches it.

Your store rooms, server or records room, plant rooms and entrances are already on camera. What is missing is a rule: which area, during which hours, and who is allowed in it. PARVEK marks each one as a zone and alerts the security desk the moment it is broken — not three weeks later when someone reviews the footage.

Rule part 01 Area

A polygon drawn on the camera view — the racks or the cabinet, not the whole frame.

+
Rule part 02 Permitted hours

Working hours, night only, or always.

+
Rule part 03 Who is allowed

Enrolled staff only, a named list, or nobody after hours.

=
Result Named alert

Security desk, in seconds, with the clip — and the area can answer back with voice and light.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is facial recognition legal where we operate?

Biometric rules vary by state — Illinois, Texas and Washington have specific biometric statutes, and several cities regulate it further. PARVEK is built so that enrolled faces and clips stay on your own appliance rather than a vendor cloud, which helps, but you should confirm your obligations with counsel. The system is equally useful with recognition turned off, using zones, timing and unknown-person detection alone.

Do we need to tell staff they are being monitored?

Notice and consent requirements depend on your state and, where applicable, your collective agreements. We would rather you raise this early than discover it at rollout — attendance and seat monitoring in particular are the features most likely to need consultation.

Can we use it for security without the operations features?

Yes, and many sites do. Capabilities are enabled individually. A building can run unknown-person, restricted zones, watchlists and tamper detection without ever turning on attendance, seat monitoring or counting.

Will this work with the cameras our landlord installed?

If they are IP or RTSP cameras and we can reach them on the network, usually yes. The assessment confirms compatibility, coverage and whether any location needs a different camera to detect reliably.

What does the security desk actually see?

A single event list across every camera, with the frame that triggered each alert, the clip around it, and the actions available for that event — acknowledge, resolve, escalate, or trigger a voice warning or light in that area.
