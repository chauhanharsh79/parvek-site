Perimeter & Intrusion

Understand what triggered a perimeter or intrusion alarm.

A boundary alarm tells you a line was crossed. It cannot tell you whether it was a person, a delivery van, a branch in the wind or a cat. PARVEK watches the same boundary on camera, raises the event with the frame that caused it, and can light the area and challenge over the speaker while the person is still standing there.

The Problem

What the workflow looks like today

The alarm arrives without a picture

A fence sensor, beam or contact reports a state change. Whoever receives it has to decide what to do with no idea what caused it.

So it gets ignored

A boundary that reports wind and wildlife all night trains the people watching it to dismiss everything. The one event that mattered arrives looking exactly like the hundred that did not.

The footage is found the next morning

By the time someone scrubs back to the right minute, the response window has closed. The video becomes a record of what happened rather than something that changed it.

How PARVEK Supports It

What changes in practice

The boundary is drawn on the camera view

Areas are marked directly on the image — the fence line, the yard, the loading apron, the gap behind the building. PARVEK raises an event when a person is inside one during the hours it is off limits.

It separates people from everything else

Person and vehicle detection run on the live stream, so movement that is not a person does not become an event. What a camera can distinguish depends on how it is placed and lit, which is what the site survey establishes.

The response happens in that area

A floodlight switched on, a recorded or live voice challenge from the speaker covering that spot, a siren where the site allows it. Light is frequently the most effective response, and it also fixes the image the camera is capturing.

The evidence is kept with the event

The frame that triggered it, the clip around it, the camera, the time and the area that was breached — held together rather than reassembled later.

Event Sources

What raises an event on this workflow

Which of these apply to your site, and which cameras can carry them, is what the site survey establishes before anything is proposed.

Camera-detected boundary crossing A person entering a marked area on the camera view during an off-limits window. This is the primary path and needs no other system.
Restricted and off-limits areas Plant rooms, stock racks, cash offices, server rooms, docks and yards — each with its own hours.
Perimeter sensors already on site Fence sensors, gate contacts, beams and radar. Where a site runs these, the useful question is which camera covers that zone, which is worked out during the survey.
Gate and vehicle events A vehicle at a gate outside permitted hours, with the frame attached.
Camera tamper and health A camera covered, moved or taken offline is itself a perimeter event, and one that is easy to miss.

Deployment

How it fits your site

It runs on the cameras you have

PARVEK reads standard RTSP and ONVIF streams, directly or through your recorder. The recorder keeps recording exactly as before.

Processing stays on site

The appliance sits on your network and analyses the streams locally. Outbound notifications only; no inbound ports opened.

Placement and light decide the result

A boundary camera pointed along a fence in usable light will do this well. One mounted high, wide and unlit will not, regardless of settings. The survey says which you have before anything is committed.

Response hardware is wired at commissioning

Speakers, floodlights and sirens are driven from relay outputs on the appliance. What they are wired to is a site decision.

Frequently Asked Questions

Do we have to replace our fence sensors or alarm panel?

No. PARVEK watches the same boundary on camera and raises its own events from what it sees. Where you already run perimeter sensors, they keep doing their job — the survey works out which camera covers each zone so the two can be used together.

Will this work at night?

It depends entirely on what the camera can see. A lit area, or a camera with usable infrared at the distance involved, works. A dark yard at the far end of a wide-angle lens does not, and no software setting changes that. This is the single most common thing the site survey finds, and it is better found before you buy than after.

Does weather set it off?

Rain, moving foliage and insects near the lens are the normal causes of boundary noise. PARVEK is looking for a person rather than for movement, which removes most of it, and the areas and hours are tuned during the proof of concept against what your site actually produces.

Can it talk to someone standing at the fence?

Where a speaker covers that area, yes — a recorded warning triggered by the event, or live talk-down from the console. Both depend on speakers being present or added at commissioning.

How do we find out whether our site is suitable?

Start with the site survey. It covers your camera list, placement, lighting and the areas you care about, and tells you what will and will not work before anything is proposed. From there, a proof of concept runs it on your own cameras.
