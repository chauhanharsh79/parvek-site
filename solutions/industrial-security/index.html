Industrial Security

Bring visual context to industrial and operational alerts.

An industrial site generates alerts all day from systems that cannot see. PARVEK adds the one thing they are missing — the picture of the area at that moment — using the cameras already installed across the plant, with the processing running on site.

Start a Site Assessment See the event sources
The Problem

What the workflow looks like today

The alert has no picture attached

A fault, a stoppage or a zone alert tells the control room a state changed. Someone still has to walk out and look, or find the camera and scrub back to the right minute.

Cameras are everywhere and watched by nobody

A plant that has accumulated cameras over a decade usually has good coverage and no one with the time to monitor it. The footage is used after incidents, not during them.

The areas that matter are the ones nobody walks past

Plant rooms, roof access, chemical stores, conveyor runs and yard corners are exactly where an unnoticed event becomes an expensive one.

How PARVEK Supports It

What changes in practice

Restricted and operational areas, watched continuously

Areas are marked on the camera view with the hours they are off limits — so a person on a conveyor run or in a plant room out of hours becomes an event rather than a thing found later.

People, vehicles and movement across the site

Person and vehicle detection, occupancy and counting, and movement between areas — the operational picture a plant has cameras for but no capacity to watch.

Response where the event is

A voice announcement in that area, a light, a relay-driven device, and the event at the control room desk with the frame attached.

Evidence that holds up afterwards

Every event carries its frame, clip, camera and time, retained on the appliance for the incident review or the report.

Deployment

How it fits your site

Built around the cameras already on the plant

Standard RTSP and ONVIF streams, read directly or through existing recorders. Mixed estates are normal and expected.

The appliance runs on site

Analysis happens locally on your network. For multi-site operations, each site runs its own appliance.

Industrial conditions are the hard part

Dust, steam, vibration, glare and night lighting all affect what a camera can resolve. The survey establishes this per camera before any capability is promised.

Safety systems stay authoritative

PARVEK supplements them with visual context. It is not a certified safety or life-safety device and does not replace one.

Frequently Asked Questions

Does PARVEK connect to our PLC or SCADA system?

Not as a shipped connector, and we will not claim otherwise. PARVEK generates its own events from the cameras on site, which is what most plants actually need. If you want operational alerts from a control system brought together with video, that is worked out with you during the survey, against the specific system you run rather than assumed.

Can this replace our fire or gas detection?

No, and it should not be positioned that way. Certified life-safety and process-safety devices remain authoritative. PARVEK can add visual context around an area, but it is not a substitute for certified detection and we do not sell it as one.

Our cameras are old and the estate is mixed. Does that matter?

Mixed estates are the normal case, and reading standard streams rather than vendor SDKs is what makes them workable. What matters more than age is placement, resolution at the distance involved and lighting. The survey tells you which of your cameras are usable as they are.

Will dust, steam and glare break it?

They degrade what any camera can resolve, and the honest answer is that it varies by location. This is exactly what a proof of concept on your own cameras is for — it shows you the real behaviour in your conditions rather than a number from someone else’s site.

We run several plants. How does that work?

Each site runs its own appliance with processing local to it, and events roll up to the people who need them with role-based access per site. Multi-site structure is part of what the survey covers.
