What It Watches For

Built around how this site actually fails

Restricted & hazardous areas

Plant rooms, electrical and generator rooms, tank farms and any space that is dangerous when someone is in it unsupervised.

Fire & smoke as a visual layer

Visible flame and smoke in high bays, material stores, plant rooms and yards where heat takes time to reach a detector. It works alongside your fire panel, not instead of it.

Fall detection on the floor

A collapse in a plant room, stairwell or on a night shift, where nobody may be watching.

Occupancy & crowding

Headcount limits in work bays and at gates, where crowding is a safety issue before it is anything else.

Perimeter & gate movement

Fence lines and service entries after hours, and vehicle movement logged at the gate.

Guard workflows

Patrol checkpoints confirmed on each round, with a supervisor alert when one is missed.

Frequently Asked Questions

Does fire detection replace our fire alarm system?

No. It is an additional visual layer that watches for visible flame and smoke in the camera view, which matters in high bays, open yards and parking where heat and smoke take time to reach a detector. It is not a listed fire-detection device and does not replace your panel.

Can it run on our existing industrial cameras?

Usually. Supported IP and RTSP cameras come onto the appliance whatever the brand or generation. Mixed estates across several generations are normal on plants and are not a problem in themselves.

Will alerts reach the floor, not just a phone?

Where speakers, horns and lights are installed and your safety rules permit, yes. A common configuration is a local voice warning in the area, the light in that area, and a silent alert plus clip to the desk.

How disruptive is deployment?

Where cameras and cabling exist and are compatible, very little — the work is assessment, appliance, configuration and tuning. Anything needing new cabling is phased around your production schedule at design stage.
