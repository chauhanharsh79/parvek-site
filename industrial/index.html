Restricted Zones & Access

A camera records the room. A zone watches it.

Your store rooms, server or records room, plant rooms and entrances are already on camera. What is missing is a rule: which area, during which hours, and who is allowed in it. PARVEK marks each one as a zone and alerts the security desk the moment it is broken — not three weeks later when someone reviews the footage.

Rule part 01 Area

A polygon drawn on the camera view — the racks or the cabinet, not the whole frame.

+
Rule part 02 Permitted hours

Working hours, night only, or always.

+
Rule part 03 Who is allowed

Enrolled staff only, a named list, or nobody after hours.

=
Result Named alert

Security desk, in seconds, with the clip — and the area can answer back with voice and light.

Frequently Asked Questions

Do we have to replace our plant cameras?

Usually not. Compatible IP and RTSP cameras in serviceable condition connect to the PARVEK appliance and gain AI, alerts and reporting. The assessment identifies what is worth keeping, what needs re-aiming and what will not give usable detection whatever we do — a camera pointed at a loading bay from 200 feet in poor light is a lighting problem, not a software one.

Does the fire detection replace our fire alarm system?

No. It is an additional visual layer that watches for visible flame and smoke in the camera view, which matters in high bays, open yards, plant rooms and parking where heat and smoke take time to reach a detector. It works alongside your fire panel, not instead of it, and it is not a listed fire-detection device.

Will PPE detection create constant false alarms?

It is scoped to the zones where PPE is actually required, rather than the whole site, and thresholds are tuned during commissioning on your own footage. Detection quality depends on camera angle and distance — we will tell you at assessment which areas can support it reliably and which cannot.

Can alerts trigger something on the floor, not just a phone?

Yes, where those devices are installed and your safety rules permit. A common configuration is a local voice warning in the area that was breached, the light in that area, and a silent alert plus clip to the security desk — keeping horns and floodlights for the perimeter.

Where does the data live, and who can see it?

On the appliance inside your facility. Faces, clips and reports stay on site, and users and roles decide who sees which cameras and which reports. Nothing is uploaded for the AI to work.

How long does a deployment take?

It depends on camera count and how much infrastructure already exists. Where cameras and cabling are in place and compatible, a site can be running detection quickly; a full build with new cameras, cabling and response devices is phased around your production schedule and agreed at design stage.
