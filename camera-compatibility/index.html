Camera Compatibility

Will your cameras work with PARVEK?

Most professional IP cameras installed in the last several years will. Here is the baseline we work to, what we recommend where identification matters, and how to get an initial answer on your own estate.

Start a Site Assessment I already have cameras
Minimum Baseline

What a camera needs to carry detection

Resolution

2 MP / 1080p or higher.

Frame rate

15 fps or higher for general analytics.

Codec

H.264 or H.265.

Streaming

RTSP, or supported ONVIF streaming.

Network

Stable IP network connectivity to the appliance.

Image quality

Usable day and night image quality for the scene.

The qualification that matters

2 MP is a compatibility minimum, not a guarantee that every AI capability will perform equally well. We assess the actual scene, lens, lighting and target distance before committing to a capability on a given camera.

Recommended

Where to go above the minimum

4 MP or higher for identification

Recommended where face identification, license plates or long-distance recognition is required. Placement, lens, lighting and target pixel density matter as much as resolution.

ONVIF Profile T preferred

For new deployments. Profile T supports H.264/H.265, metadata, events and other advanced video-streaming functions.

    Frequently Asked Questions

    Does 2 MP mean every capability will work?

    No — and this is the most important thing on this page. 2 MP is a compatibility minimum, not a performance promise. A 2 MP camera covering a doorway at 15 feet will support far more than the same camera covering a yard at 200 feet. We assess the actual scene, lens, lighting and target distance before committing to a capability on a given camera.

    My cameras are from a brand not on your list. Do they work?

    Probably, if they stream RTSP or supported ONVIF. The vendor list is representative of professional IP cameras we see regularly, not a compatibility whitelist. Send the models and we will check.

    What if some cameras qualify and others do not?

    That is the normal outcome. Capabilities are configured per camera, so the ones covering your dock can run counting and restricted areas while older cameras elsewhere simply record. You are not obliged to bring the whole estate to one standard.

    Do you need physical access to check?

    Not for the initial review. Makes, models and a screenshot of the camera views tell us most of it. A site assessment confirms the rest — network path, lighting through a real night, and mounting.

    What about analog cameras?

    They need an encoder to produce an IP stream, or replacement. If your estate is largely analog, that is worth knowing before anything else — tell us and we will be straight about whether this is the right time.
